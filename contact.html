Philippine Climate Allies & Resources

TINDIG stands in solidarity with frontline civil society organizations defending our environment and communities:

Youth Advocates for Climate Action Philippines (YACAP)

The Philippine chapter of Fridays for Future, uniting youth activists and indigenous defenders across Luzon, Visayas, and Mindanao.

Focus: Youth Strikes, Climate Education & Policy Advocacy

Greenpeace Philippines

Leading investigations on climate accountability, plastic pollution reduction, and renewable energy adoption in Southeast Asia.

Focus: Carbon Majors Inquiry, Ocean Sanctuaries & Fossil Fuel Phaseout

Kalikasan People's Network for the Environment

Grassroots network advocating for the defense of ancestral lands, environmental defenders, and sustainable ecological stewardship.

Focus: Land Rights, Anti-Reclamation & Community Resilience

Center for Energy, Ecology, and Development (CEED)

Think-do tank generating rigorous energy research to advance a just energy transition away from dirty coal and imported LNG.

Focus: Clean Energy Research & Verde Island Passage Protection

Frequently Asked Questions

Common inquiries about our academic advocacy platform, data sources, and youth organizing:

TINDIG is an open educational and advocacy resource. Student organizations are free to project the platform during environmental summits, share the My Climate Pledge tracker in campus orientations, or use our documentary photos for non-commercial climate literacy campaigns.
All data points are sourced directly from peer-reviewed institutional publications, including the Philippine Atmospheric, Geophysical and Astronomical Services Administration (PAGASA), the IPCC Sixth Assessment Report (AR6), and the Philippine Climate Change Commission.
Always partner with the local Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) or community-based marine sanctuaries. Planting the wrong species (e.g., planting terrestrial trees in saline tidal zones) can disrupt ecosystems; indigenous Bakawan (Rhizophora) propagules must be planted in suitable muddy intertidal flats.
