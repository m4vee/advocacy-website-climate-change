The Three Frontlines We Face

Climate change is not a theoretical forecast in the Philippine archipelago—it is an everyday disruption to classrooms, livelihood, and family homes.

Frontline 01 · Extreme Weather

Super Typhoons & Stronger Surges

Warming sea-surface temperatures in the Western Pacific rapidly supercharge typhoons before landfall, leaving coastal provinces like Leyte, Samar, and Cagayan facing wind gusts exceeding 280 km/h.

Read the typhoon chronology
Frontline 02 · Inundation

Chronic Tidal & Urban Flooding

Low-lying centers such as Malabon, Navotas, and Bulacan experience daily "lubog" even on sunny days during full moon high tides, accelerated by unchecked groundwater extraction and silted river channels.

Examine flood data
Frontline 03 · Heat Index

Urban Heat Islands & Crop Failure

Concrete congestion in Metro Manila and Central Luzon creates suffocating heat pockets. In agricultural belts, prolonged El Niño droughts dry irrigation canals and plunge farming families into debt.

The Spirit of Bayanihan

Why This Is Our Shared Fight

In the Philippines, resilience is often romanticized—praised in photographs of children smiling through waist-deep waters. But true resilience isn't passive acceptance; it is Bayanihan: the ancient Filipino custom of lifting an entire house together upon communal shoulders.

When one barangay is washed away, our shared food supplies dwindle. When typhoon seasons stretch longer, our school calendars rupture. We refuse to let climate loss become our normal. Through collective digital storytelling, policy advocacy, and community action, we transform shared vulnerability into unified power.
